# The journey: plan for tiles 3 to 7 and the campaign endless mode

*Written 13 November 2026 from your outline and map sketch. Nothing in this file is built yet.
It is a plan for you to correct. Sections 1 to 6 need no knowledge of the code. Sections 7 to 9
name files and are for whoever builds it.*

## 1. Summary

Your outline turns the lessons into one journey across a map of seven tiles. Two families walk
it, split, and meet again. Five new lessons and three new free plays are needed.

Most of the world you describe already exists in the first Endless level: lancewood,
speargrass, harakeke, the kākā, the kākāpō, the mast year, the kōkako, the Forest Seed and the
tour of a 2×2 land. What does not exist is everything that makes it a story:

1. **A campaign record.** Today no level knows what happened in another. A generational story
   needs a saved record of who is alive, how each herd fared and what the player grew.
2. **A map screen** where the player sees the tiles and chooses west or north.
3. **Travel.** A named party that walks off one land and onto the next, and in the last free
   play moves with the camera around the 2×2 land.
4. **Rules the simulation does not have yet**, such as kea harrying an eagle. Each must be a
   real rule of the world, switched on by the level, with a sign the player can see.
5. **Words, names and art that are yours to make**: the riddle, the crested moa's lore, names,
   and several sprites and sounds.

The build is seven stages. Each ends with something you can play from the menu. I recommend
you play the first two new lessons before the rest are built, because nobody has yet played a
Chapters lesson by hand.

There are fourteen decisions in section 10. Two matter most: how the chick is taken without
breaking the rule every lesson teaches, and how long a sitting the free plays should be.

## 3. What can carry from one level to the next

This is the part that makes the story generational, so it is worth being exact about what can
honestly vary and what cannot.

**What cannot vary.** A lesson can only be won with its leads alive, as the five years already
work. The leads are Koukou and the baby in the west, and Pukepuke in the north. So those
three always reach tile 7. Without that, the plot has no tile 7.

**What can vary, and will be remembered:**

| Remembered | Decided by | Where it shows later |
|---|---|---|
| Which of Mama, Papa and the neighbours are alive | The eagle and the winters on tiles 5, 6 and 6f | Tile 7's second nest. Two elders make a nest and may lay. One elder gets a wintering place. None means one nest only. The words change to match |
| Pukepuke's younger siblings | Whether Mama and Papa raise chicks in 6 and 6f | They walk onto tile 7 as part of the clan |
| How the bush moa and the kākāpō fared | 4f | Their starting numbers in the two western quarters of 7f |
| How the giant moa, the kākā and the upland clan fared | 5, 6 and 6f | Their starting numbers in the two eastern quarters of 7f |
| How bountiful the forest was left | 4f | The forest in 7f's north-west quarter in the mast year |
| Whether the new chick is found again | Year 2 of 7f | The family named on the closing card |
| How often the player acted with no hint | Every lesson | The closing card of each lesson and of the journey |

At the end of 7f's fourth year the closing card names the family: who set out, who arrived,
who was lost and to what, and who was born on the way. A family tree panel can show the same
at any time, since every bird already knows its mother and father.

## 4. The five lessons

Each lesson follows the pattern of the built ones. It teaches one rule of the world, gives one
lever, and shows one consequence. The simulation decides every outcome. The lesson sets the
stage, measures and speaks. Hints come as a ladder: nothing, then a question, then the
instruction. The last season has no hints at all.

All titles below are placeholders.

### Tile 3. Into the bush

- **Story.** Koukou and the baby walk in from the east into forest they do not know. A herd
  of little bush moa is feeding on the same fern. The baby goes hungry beside them. In spring
  they hear a booming in the night from the north.
- **The rule.** Moa crowded on the same forest food each get less. This rule is already in
  the simulation. It is switched on in the first Endless level and nothing shows it.
- **The lever.** Lancewood, which only bush moa eat. Planted away from the baby, it draws the
  bush moa off.
- **The clue in the world.** A few wild lancewood grow on the land, and bush moa can be seen
  choosing them before the tool is given. A small mark shows over a moa whose meal was cut
  short by crowding.
- **The year.** Summer: the problem appears. Autumn: the Pouākai comes, and the storm still
  hides a moa, as tile 1 taught. Winter: more bush moa come into the refuge and the crowding
  is at its worst, so one stand is not enough. Spring: the booming, and the walk north.
- **To build.** The crowding mark and an event for it, so a hint ladder can open. Everything
  else is level writing.

### Tile 4. The night parrots

- **Story.** Koukou and the baby meet the kākāpō. An old bird sets a riddle. Its answer is
  that the rimu will fruit heavily next summer, and a flock that is ready will breed.
- **The rule.** Kākāpō breed only in a mast year. The mast can be seen coming a year ahead.
- **The lever.** The Forest Seed. A grove sown by the kākāpō's courts this year bears in the
  mast next year.
- **The clue in the world.** Rimu carry green fruit in the year before a mast. This is the
  riddle's answer drawn on the land, so a player who looks can solve it with no hint.
- **Two years.** Year one: the riddle, the green fruit, sow the groves, bring the flock
  through winter. Year two: the mast. The males boom, the hens come to the courts, and chicks
  hatch if the fruit is close enough.
- **The riddle as a hint ladder.** The riddle itself is the first rung. A plainer line comes
  a season later. The instruction comes by winter. The record shows at which rung the player
  sowed.
- **To build.** A way for a lesson to set the mast year. The green fruit. A check that a sown
  grove bears within a year. The riddle's words are yours.

### Tile 5. The crowded forest

- **Story.** Pukepuke, his parents and Koukou's parents go north into higher country. The
  cold drives them down to the forest edge in winter, and a large flock of kākā has fed there
  all autumn. At the end, Koukou's parents turn for home.
- **The rule.** The forest is shared. Winter food has to be grown a season ahead, at the
  place the family will winter.
- **The lever.** A grove of harakeke, planted in autumn so it is grown by winter.
- **The second tool.** Patches of speargrass stand on the tops. The player cannot plant one
  but can pick one up and set it down, and the family follows its favoured food. Moved
  downhill in steps, the patches lead the family to the grove.
- **To build.** A rule by which a feeding flock leaves less on the forest floor, with a sign
  for it. A growing-in time for planted harakeke, so that planting ahead matters. Moving a
  standing patch is already built for pātōtara.
- **A caution.** This lesson carries more than the others: the shared forest, planning ahead
  and leading with food. I suggest the grove is the one rule that is hinted and tested, and
  that moving speargrass is left for the player to find.

### Tile 6. The crested one

- **Story.** Pukepuke, Mama and Papa go north again and meet a crested moa, an old bird of
  the high country, who tells them what it knows.
- **The rule.** Two patches of a moa's favoured plant, with the pair near, found a nest.
  This is built, and the endless variant "The Tops" already uses it.
- **The lever.** Speargrass, now plantable.
- **The year.** Summer: found a nest and Mama lays. Autumn: kea come for the egg, and the
  family at the nest defeats the raid, as tile 2 taught. Winter: the grove, as tile 5 taught.
  Spring: the chick is a year old and the family turns west.
- **The lore.** Three or four lines from the crested moa, each released by something the
  player does. They can point ahead to the giant moa, the eagle's roost and the meeting.
  The words are yours.
- **To build.** A sign that one patch is down and a second would found a nest. A crested
  moa that can be told apart on screen.

### Tile 7. Where the paths meet

- **Story.** Koukou and the baby walk in from the south. Pukepuke's family walks in from the
  east. They meet. Two nests are made, and Koukou and Pukepuke's chick hatches and is named.
- **No new rule.** Lancewood keeps the bush moa off the nesting ground. Speargrass founds the
  nests. Kea come for the eggs. The storm hides the hunted. Every rule of the journey is in
  play at once, with the fewest hints of any lesson.
- **It changes with the record.** See section 3.
- **It is played on the same land as 7f.** The nests founded here are standing when the
  free play opens.
- **Unlocked** when tiles 4 and 6 are both won.

## 5. The three free plays

All three use the form of "The Tops": the same animals live on from year to year, moa breed
by their calendars, nothing is protected by a floor, and each season sets a few small goals.

### 4f. The mast years

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | Eight years. Mast in years 4 and 8 |
| Cast | Koukou, the baby, bush moa, kākāpō, the Pouākai |
| Tools | Lancewood, Forest Seed, the storm. The Rimu Berry Scramble in mast years |
| Pressure | The cold deepens each year and the forest band shrinks from the top |
| Won | At the end of year 8, if the forest is more bountiful than at the start and the kākāpō are still there |
| Lost | Koukou or the baby is lost, or the kākāpō die out |

A new measure, **forest bounty**, counts the fruiting trees on the land. It sits beside the
mauri ring with a mark at its starting value, so the player can see the goal all game.

Koukou and the baby cannot breed here, since there are no other upland moa. That gives the
story its reason to go north again.

### 6f. The giant moa

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | Eight years, to match 4f |
| Cast | Pukepuke, Mama, Papa and their young, upland moa that arrive over the pass, a small herd of South Island giant moa, kākā, kea, the Pouākai |
| Tools | Speargrass, harakeke, the storm |
| Won | At the end of year 8, if the clan has reached its target and the giant moa are no fewer than at the start |
| Lost | Pukepuke is lost, or the giant moa die out |

The balance here needs no new rule. The giant moa lays one egg every two or three years and
takes eight years to grow up. Two rules the player already knows press on it. A crowded
winter forest feeds everyone less. And a Pouākai whose chosen bird is hidden turns to another.
A player who hides every one of their own birds sends the eagle to the giant moa.

## 7. What has to be built

Every new behaviour goes behind a level switch, as now, so no existing level changes. Each
stage is checked by replaying the existing levels and comparing their replay hashes.

### 7.1 The campaign layer (new file `mauri_campaign.js`)

- **The record.** Stored beside the player's progress under its own key, with a version
  number and a reset. It holds the named birds (alive or lost, cause, age, parents), the
  final numbers of each herd from each free play, the forest bounty, and the learner's
  unaided counts.
- **Reading it.** When a level starts, the record becomes story flags and word tokens, which
  the chapters engine already has. A reaction can say `if: { flag: 'papa_lost' }` and a line
  can say `{$elders}`. No new condition words are needed.
- **Writing it.** A level lists what it hands on in an `outcome` block. The runner writes it
  at the win.
- **A carried cast.** A cast entry marked `carry: true` is set down from the record at its
  remembered age and size.
- **Starting without history.** Every level must also start from a default record, so any
  tile can be opened alone for testing or for a class.

### 7.2 The map screen

A new first section in the menu, "The journey", drawn as your sketch: tiles, the two path
lines, and each tile shown as done, open or locked. The existing Stories, Chapters, Endless
and Classic sections stay as they are for comparison. After the five years, the win card
offers both ways on: west with Koukou or north with Pukepuke.

### 7.3 Travel

- **Between tiles.** A lesson ends with the party walking to the edge that faces the next
  tile. The next lesson opens with them walking in from the opposite edge. Walking out is
  built already, for grown young leaving over the pass.
- **Around the 2×2 land.** This is the largest piece of engine work. The first Endless
  level's move between quarters is tied to its own year engine. It has to be separated out so
  a chapter can say which quarter it is played on, and so the travelling party is kept while
  everything else is set down fresh. The tour order is already a setting, so starting in the
  south-east needs no new code.
- **Tile 7 and 7f share one land.** Tile 7 is the south-east quarter of 7f's world, made from
  the same seed.

## 9. Testing for different players

The method is the one used for "The Tops" and the Clues sketches.

- **Every lesson:** stand-in players who are careful, slow, late, or who do nothing, on six
  seeds. The careful one must mostly act before any hint. The one who does nothing must lose.
  The last season must log no hint.
- **New stand-ins for the new tools:** one who only plants lancewood, one who only sows
  forest, one who plants the grove too late, one who hides every bird in 6f.
- **Every free play:** eight years, six seeds, each stand-in. I will report runs ended early,
  the leads' fate, herd sizes and the win measure, as a table, and you set the targets.
- **The record:** tile 7 and 7f started from five written records, from "everyone lived and
  both forests thrived" to "both elders lost and the kākāpō barely held".
- **Nothing old changes:** every existing level's replay hash is compared at every stage.
- **What this cannot show** is whether a child reads the green fruit, or cares that Papa was
  lost. That needs people, which is why the pause after stage C matters.

## 11. Risks

1. **Volume.** Five lessons and three free plays is more than the whole Chapters set so far.
   The kit in 7.5 is what keeps it affordable.
2. **The tour.** Separating the move between quarters from the first Endless level's year
   engine touches the oldest and largest file. That level's replay hash is the guard.
3. **Slow breeders over eight years.** "The Tops" needed several rounds of tuning before nine
   years held together. Each free play will need the same.
4. **Story against the same-rule test.** The taking of the chick, speaking birds and a
   findable roost are story devices. The plan keeps them outside play or marks them imagined.
5. **Length.** A season is 45 seconds, so a year is three minutes. End to end the journey is
   about an hour and three quarters of running time, before pauses and reading. More than
   half of that is the three free plays. This is worked out from season lengths, not timed.
6. **No players yet.** Everything above is designed against stand-ins.
